Cryptography is the invisible infrastructure of the digital age. Every time you log into your bank account, send an encrypted text message, tap a credit card, or update your phone's operating system, complex mathematical algorithms are working silently in the background to ensure security, privacy, and trust.
Historically, cryptography was a tool used almost exclusively by governments, militaries, and spies to pass hidden written messages. Today, it has evolved into a foundational pillar of computer science that secures trillions of dollars in daily global commerce and protects the private data of billions of people.
To understand how modern digital societies function, it is essential to understand how cryptography works—from its ancient roots to the quantum-resistant algorithms of tomorrow.
